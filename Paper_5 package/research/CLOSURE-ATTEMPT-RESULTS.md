# Targeted closure attempt: proved findings and remaining assertion

27 October 2026. This is a statement of mathematical results, not an export
of private reasoning or an assertion that every conceivable method was tried.

The exact target was the forced final prime, rather than repetition of
completed finite searches. The revised Appendix D proves the following.

1. For every prime prefix, any completion has the unique quotient
   M=ceil(A/B) and unique possible last prime q=1+(A-1)/(MB-A).
   The divisibility, primality and ordering tests together are equivalent
   to a Lehmer counterexample; they have not been excluded universally.
2. Exact divisibility already forces the displayed gcd and full valuation
   identities. Reimposing those identities cannot independently close the gap.
3. Deleting the largest prime cannot send a hypothetical Lehmer solution to
   another Lehmer solution, even with a changed integer quotient. The quotient
   after deletion lies strictly between consecutive integers M-1 and M.
4. The two-prime replacement equation factors as
   (Cp-MB)(Cq-MB)=MAB-C. Its inverse requires congruences, ordering and primality.
   Monotonicity of the product along the real hyperbola does not prove the
   existence of a second prime recovery.
5. For the extreme divisor u=1, every integral recovery has q=Ap. If A>1
   this is composite. Thus this explicit replacement construction fails to
   preserve primality. Other compatible divisors remain unexcluded.

The separate exact check refutes the examined pivot manuscript's Lemma 6.40
as stated. This finding prevents using that lemma as an established uniform
descent input; it is not a resolution of Lehmer's conjecture.

Outcome: no new all-prefix exclusion, no new all-factor-count exclusion,
and no full-conjecture proof. The manuscript is suitable for review only
with its accurately stated finite-results scope. Publication as a full
resolution would misstate the proved result.
